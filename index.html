Filadelfia Church · Stockholm, Sweden

It all began at Filadelfia Church in Stockholm, at a Johnny Chang event in Sweden. In a place filled with unfamiliar faces, there was one person who somehow caught my attention.

I didn't know her name. I didn't know her story. I didn't even know who she was. But there was something about her that stood out. She carried herself with a boldness and courage that was hard to ignore.

I remember watching her from a distance and wondering, “Who is this girl?”

At the time, I had no idea that the stranger who caught my attention that day was Sara — or that this simple moment of curiosity would become the beginning of our journey.

After the event

The hall was still full — people talking, taking photos, meeting each other. She had a certain boldness about her, but she was shy to ask the guest speaker for an interview. So I walked over to ask who she was and why she was there.

“There she is again...”

Their paths cross

So I walked over and asked. It turned into an ordinary conversation — nothing dramatic, just two people introducing themselves.

The Instagram exchange

Somewhere in the middle of that conversation, we ended up trading Instagram handles. A small, ordinary thing. Neither of us thought much of it.

Later that night

I kept scrolling. Post after post, trying to work out who this person actually was.

I was only trying to figure out who she was. I had no idea I was looking at someone who would become such an important part of my story.

Victory Outreach Stockholm

A few days later she came to see Victory Outreach Stockholm for herself. She showed up alone, the way she seemed to do most things, and walked through the front doors into a room full of people she had never met. She shook hands, asked her questions, and listened while people told her what this place had done for their lives. She didn't look nervous. She looked like someone who wanted to understand.

Click next.

Our first call

Then she let me have her number. Our first call was early in the morning, standing in front of a lake with the trees still holding the cold. We talked about politics, about our families, about school stories neither of us had told in years. An hour disappeared. I hung up and realized I had been smiling the whole time.

Getting to know her

After that we were always on the phone — texting all day, calling at night. I learned the small things first: lemons, onions, garlic in everything, sushi, Mexican food, steak medium well, and the spicier the better. I learned she loves the smell of tea more than almost anyone I know — rooibos, vanilla, ginger, mint, peppermint, chamomile, cinnamon, black tea with milk. And somewhere in all of it I noticed something bigger: the things she valued were the things I valued. We were getting closer without either of us announcing it.

The house she's been picturing

Then she told me about the house she has been picturing her whole life. Two storeys. Seven children running through it. A gym with a big mirror lit all the way around, a walk-in closet, flowers in every room, and canvases waiting for her to paint on. She described it so plainly, like she had already walked through it a hundred times. I listened, and the builder in me thought: I could make that.
